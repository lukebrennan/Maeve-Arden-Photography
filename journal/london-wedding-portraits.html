Finding a quiet moment in London

City texture, a little movement and somewhere to pause. Portraits can be part of the journey, rather than a long detour.

In these notes

Keep the route shortLook for calm within the cityCheck access before the dayMake room for the unexpectedStay connected to the celebration

Keep the route short

Choose one or two portrait locations near your venue, then plan a simple route. Long journeys can quickly eat into a wedding schedule. A doorway, a stretch of pavement or a sheltered courtyard may be all you need.

Look for calm within the city

Interesting light and texture are often more useful than a recognisable landmark. Look for open shade, pale stone, reflections and quiet corners. A place you can enjoy for a few minutes is more valuable than a busy backdrop you have to fight.

Check access before the day

A public-looking space may still have photography restrictions. Confirm access with the venue or landowner, especially for indoor locations or a larger setup. Keep a nearby alternative so one unavailable spot doesn’t upset the plan.

Make room for the unexpected

Traffic, crowds and weather can change the feel of a route. Build in a little breathing room and keep the plan adaptable. If a location is crowded, use a closer frame, move to a quieter corner, or return to the venue.

Stay connected to the celebration

Portraits can be a brief pause together before you return to your guests. Agree a comfortable amount of time in advance. The city gives you the setting; your connection gives the photographs their meaning.
